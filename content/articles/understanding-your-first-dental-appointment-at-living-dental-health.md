**Your first comprehensive appointment at Living Dental Health starts with a conversation and an examination; any needed X-rays help complete the picture.** The routine new-patient cleaning is scheduled separately so the team can recommend the right type of care for your teeth and gums. You will have time to discuss the findings and next steps.

If you are booking because something hurts, say that when you call. An appointment for a specific problem may be organized differently from a routine new-patient examination.

## What should you bring to your first visit?

Bring a current medication list, relevant medical and dental history, insurance information if you have it, and requested paperwork. Tell the team about allergies, health changes, and any concerns you want addressed. A parent or guardian must accompany patients under 18.

### Arrange your previous records

Ask your previous dental office to transfer recent records and X-rays. The [ADA’s guidance on dental X-rays](https://www.mouthhealthy.org/all-topics-a-z/x-rays) explains that existing images may be useful and that new imaging should be based on individual needs. A new office does not automatically mean every image must be repeated.

### Bring your questions, too

Write down anything you do not want to forget: a sensitive tooth, bleeding gums, a change in your bite, or concerns about cost. If you have avoided care for a while, simply tell us. The starting point is where you are now.

## What does the comprehensive dental exam include?

[Dr. Andy Engel](/about) reviews your concerns and dental history and evaluates your teeth, gums, bite, and overall oral condition. Imaging is recommended when it can provide information needed for your care. The [MedlinePlus overview of adult dental care](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/001957.htm) describes the role of examinations and professional care in identifying problems.

You should leave understanding what was found, which issues need attention, and what questions remain. You can ask to see the area being discussed and have unfamiliar terms explained.

## Why is the cleaning a separate appointment?

A routine preventive cleaning and treatment for gum disease are different services. Examining your mouth first helps the team plan the appropriate appointment instead of assuming every patient needs the same cleaning.

Our [guide to regular cleanings, deep cleanings, and periodontal maintenance](/articles/not-all-dental-cleanings-are-the-same-understanding-the-difference-between-prophy-and-perio-cleanings) explains the distinction. You can also review our [general dentistry services](/general-dentistry).

## What happens if you need treatment?

Ask for an explanation of the recommended care, alternatives, priorities, expected fees, and follow-up. A treatment plan should help you make decisions, rather than leave you with a list of unfamiliar procedures.

### Understand the estimate before deciding

If you have insurance, ask how benefits affect the estimate and what remains uncertain. An estimate is not a guarantee of payment by your insurer. Our [dental insurance guide](/articles/navigating-dental-insurance-the-importance-of-choosing-the-right-dentist-over-an-insurance-plan) explains the questions worth asking.

## How can you make the first visit easier?

Tell the team about anxiety before the appointment. You can agree on a signal for a break and ask for an explanation before each step. For a smaller first step, read about the [complimentary meet and greet and anxiety support](/articles/dental-anxiety-and-sedation-options).
