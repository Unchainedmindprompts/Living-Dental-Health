**Choose a dentist in Bend by comparing the services you need, relevant experience, communication, appointment access, and costs.** A good fit is an office where you can ask questions, understand your options, and feel comfortable returning for care. Living Dental Health welcomes new patients ages 12 and older.

Moving, changing insurance, or returning after years away can make this decision feel bigger than it should. You do not need to become a dental expert. You need a few useful questions and clear answers.

## What should you look for in a new dentist?

### Care that matches your needs

Start with the reason you are looking. Do you need an ongoing home for exams and cleanings, help with a missing tooth, or a team that takes dental anxiety seriously? Ask which services are offered in the office and when a specialist referral might be recommended.

Living Dental Health provides [general dentistry](/general-dentistry), [dental implants](/dental-implants), and [cosmetic dentistry](/cosmetic-dentistry). Discuss your situation before assuming a particular treatment is right for you.

### Relevant experience and clear explanations

Look beyond broad claims such as “advanced” or “the best.” Ask about experience with the care you are considering, what alternatives exist, and why a treatment is being recommended.

[Dr. Andrew W. Engel](/about) graduated from OHSU School of Dentistry in 1998 and has practiced in Bend since that year. He founded Living Dental Health in 2013. Those are different milestones: the dentist’s experience predates the practice.

### Practical access and a comfortable conversation

Check the location, hours, and availability before booking. Ask how urgent problems are handled, including outside normal office hours. An emergency policy is more useful than assuming someone will always be available immediately.

The [American Dental Association’s guide to choosing a dentist](https://www.mouthhealthy.org/dental-care/how-to-choose-a-dentist/) recommends considering access, communication, benefits, and an introductory conversation. Bring your concerns, including any past experiences that make visits difficult.

## How do insurance and costs affect the choice?

Ask whether the dentist participates in your **specific plan and network**, not simply whether the office can submit a claim. Different policies can have different benefits even when the insurer’s name is the same. The [ADA explains the differences between dental plan types](https://www.mouthhealthy.org/dental-care/types-of-dental-plans).

For practical questions to ask before treatment, read our [guide to dental insurance and out-of-network care](/articles/navigating-dental-insurance-the-importance-of-choosing-the-right-dentist-over-an-insurance-plan). The office can also explain its [in-office dental plan](/patient-info#dental-plan) if you do not have insurance.
